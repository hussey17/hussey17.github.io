Infrastructure Agnostic Maternal Health Triage: Deploying Fine Tuned and Quantized Small Language Models in Low Resource Settings

CS 5606: Designing Human-Centred AI

Paper Presentation Materials
Details
  • Study design. We built 150 synthetic clinical dictations in code-switched Roman Urdu, grounded in published maternal-health vocabularies and WHO antenatal care guidelines, and seeded with realistic speech-recognition errors. We compared three small language models (Gemma 2 2B, Llama 3.2 3B and Qwen 2.5 3B) with Gemini 2.5 Flash at extracting structured medical records from these dictations. We then fine-tuned the small models with LoRA on 120 cases and compressed them to 4-bit precision. A Gemini-based judge scored every record on a 90-point clinical rubric across eight dimensions, validated against two clinicians on a stratified sample (r = 0.72).
  • Key findings. Off-the-shelf small models reached 75 to 82% of the cloud model's score, falling furthest behind on resolving euphemisms and transcription errors. After fine-tuning, all three matched or exceeded the cloud model, with Gemma 2B reaching 104.7% of its score. At 4-bit precision, Qwen 3B kept 98.8% of its fine-tuned score and matched the cloud baseline (62.14 vs 62.34 out of 90) within 2 GB of memory. Every model found implicit, scattered red flags much harder to detect than explicit ones.
  • Implications. Fine-tuned, quantized small models fit within the memory of budget smartphones and can generate maternal health records offline, keeping patient data on the device where internet access is unreliable. Future work will validate these models on real health-worker recordings and larger test sets, and develop targeted training for detecting implicit red flags.

Modeling Agent Performance and Reviewer Interaction in Software Development: An Empirical Study

CS 365: AI-Driven Software Engineering

Submitted to the MSR 2026 Mining Challenge

Paper Code
Details
  • Study design. Using the AIDev-Pop dataset, we analysed about 32,000 closed pull requests written by five coding agents (OpenAI Codex, Claude Code, Cursor, Copilot and Devin) across task types such as fixes, features, tests and CI. For each agent and task type, we computed merge rate, mean resolution time, code churn and review-comment volume. After Shapiro–Wilk normality tests, we used Spearman's rank correlation to relate success to speed (RQ1) and code changes to reviewer interaction (RQ2).
  • Key findings. The link between merging and resolution speed depends on the agent. Most agent–task pairs (69%) were negatively correlated, with Devin's merged pull requests resolving faster, while Claude Code's merged CI pull requests took longer (r = +0.71) and Codex showed almost no relationship. Larger code changes drew more review comments in 82.8% of 58 agent–task combinations, most consistently for Copilot (mean r = 0.45).
  • Implications. Merge rate is a more reliable signal than resolution time when choosing an agent for a task, and code churn is a reasonable indicator of the review effort an agent's pull request will need.

Unpacking Decline and Dominance: A Machine Learning Framework to Investigate Performance Divergence Between Two Elite Football Clubs

Sports Analytics

Paper Code
Details
  • Study design. I collected every Premier League match that Manchester United and Manchester City failed to win over the six seasons after Sir Alex Ferguson's retirement (2013/14 to 2018/19), excluding matches with nothing left to play for. Each match is described by matchday, venue, goals for and against, Elo difference, rest days, pre-match betting odds and the opponent's final position, with expected goals (xG) difference and possession added from 2015/16. I applied k-means clustering, choosing k through silhouette analysis, to each era, each manager's tenure and City's title-winning seasons.
  • Key findings. From 2015/16 to 2018/19, United failed to win 71 of the included matches against City's 46. United's failures split into two profiles: defeats by top-four rivals, and dropped points against mid-table and lower sides despite 62.6% possession and an xG difference of only about 0.5, which points to poor chance creation. City's dropped points in Guardiola's later seasons came against lower-half sides they still out-created (xG difference 0.86), which suggests finishing variance rather than a structural weakness. Earlier City slip-ups often came against better-rested opponents.
  • Implications. Clustering non-wins on match context and xG separates structural weaknesses from short-term variance, a distinction that league tables and simple averages miss. Here it ties United's decline to weak chance creation and repeated squad rebuilding across managers rather than to bad luck.

Cobalt Events: Campus Event Discovery App

CS 360: Software Engineering

Code Android APK
  • Android app built in Java on Firebase, with separate roles for students, organisers and administrators.
  • Students browse a live, searchable feed with filters and a "Closing Soon" section, register for events and view venues on an interactive map.
  • Organisers draft events, pin the exact venue on a map, submit events for approval and scan attendees' QR tickets at check-in.
  • Administrators approve organisers and events, review payment details and track attendance and revenue.
  • Push notifications keep users updated on approvals and event changes.
